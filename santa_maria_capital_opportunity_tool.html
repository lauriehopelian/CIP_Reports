⚠ Fiscal Context: The City is in its 15th consecutive year of structural General Fund deficit. The 2024-26 budget depletes three reserve funds (General Fund, Measure U, LEAF). 93.4% of capital spending comes from Enterprise Funds (Water, Wastewater, Solid Waste, Transit), Growth Mitigation fees, Gas Tax, and Measure A — not the General Fund. General Fund capital is tightly constrained at $3.5M (FY24-25) and $2.8M (FY25-26). Score accordingly: the largest opportunities are in Utilities, not General Fund.
📖How to Read the Scoring

Score 5 — Act Now: Funded, design complete or active, bid date known or construction underway.

Score 4 — Get Positioned: Funded, budget approved, design beginning or near-term trigger confirmed.

Score 3 — Watch Actively: Budgeted in 2025-26 or multi-year program with confirmed funding trajectory.

Score 2 — Monitor: Future-year project (2026-28 CIP) with no active design or dependent on future bond/grant.

Score 1 — Closeout/Equipment: Fleet or equipment replacement; no design services needed.

Key: Utilities Enterprise Fund projects are the richest opportunity. Water/Wastewater capital is $36M (FY24-25) alone. Solid Waste Disposal's Los Flores IWMF is a multi-decade, $100M+ construction program. General Fund projects are small and tightly constrained.

🎯Projects with Clearest Near-Term Paths

WWTP Upgrade (Wastewater) — Regulatory mandate (Regional Water Quality Control Board General Permit, enrolled Feb 2022). Total estimated cost exceeds $300M. Master Plan underway. Bond issuance forecast for FY 2027-28. Environmental/civil engineers and WWTP process specialists should be engaged now on master plan and feasibility work.

Sports Complex at Depot & Battles — 19 acres purchased. Budget narrative says "construction is to begin this year" (2024-25). Measure U capital ($1.2M) committed. Site development, civil, and sports facility contractors should be on-radar for RFPs.

Water Distribution CIP — $32.2M budgeted in FY24-25 (Water Fund). Named projects: Stowell/Black Waterline, McClelland Waterline, Reservoir 4 Roof, Well #6 Intertie, Well #15, Well Site Generator Enclosures, Corporation Yard. Active procurement across multiple packages.

Los Flores IWMF (Landfill) — $16.7M budgeted in FY24-25 Solid Waste Disposal Fund. Environmental permitting, civil, liner construction. Bond issuance FY 2026-27. Multi-phase program through 2032.

Fire Training Facility — Budgeted FY 2025-26 in four-year CIP. Architects and fire facility specialists should be positioning now.

⚠️Structural Constraints to Watch

General Fund is depleted. The City draws $21.3M from reserves in FY24-25. Only $3.5M in GF capital FY24-25 ($2.8M in FY25-26). Eight requested GF capital projects were unfunded ($2.5M). Do not expect GF-funded construction beyond what's in this budget.

Capital is nearly entirely Enterprise-funded. 93.4% of capital comes from Water, Wastewater, Solid Waste, Transit, Gas Tax, and Growth Mitigation. These funds are healthy and actively spending — they are where the capital market is.

ARPA deadline was December 31, 2024. All ARPA funds had to be spent or encumbered by that date. Any ARPA-funded capital projects were either completed or encumbered before this budget was built.

Future-year CIP projects (2026-28) are unfunded in this budget. Enhanced Roadway Maintenance, Depot/Railroad Bikeway, College Drive Bikeway, East Main Rehab, BRT, Fire Station 2 relocation, Pocket Parks, and ADA at Simas Field are all in the 4-year plan but not appropriated until 2026+.

WWTP costs may exceed $300M. Full scope unknown until Master Plan complete. This is the City's largest single capital exposure and will require rate increases and bonding — a generational infrastructure program.

🚩Red Flags / False Positives

Mobile Equipment Replacement ($18.5M over 2 years) — 46 pieces of fleet equipment (refuse trucks, vehicles, etc.). This is equipment procurement, not design or construction. Fleet vendors and dealers — not engineering or construction firms — are the targets here.

Growth Mitigation Fund ($8.2M FY24-25) — This is a pool for multiple uses (traffic, rec/parks, public safety, water/wastewater impact fees). Not a single project. Funds are allocated to specific projects as triggered by development — not a standalone bid opportunity.

Gas Tax & TDA ($5.4M FY24-25) — Annual preventive street maintenance. Largely allocated to recurring road overlay and maintenance contracts. Limited new design opportunity; focused on contractors already on rotating maintenance contracts.

Wastewater CIP ($4.25M FY24-25) — The large $14.7M FY23-24 wastewater capital budget dropped dramatically in FY24-25. This reflects timing — the WWTP Master Plan is underway but major construction won't begin until 2027-28 per the rate model.

Park Acquisition Fund ($150K FY24-25) — Minimal appropriation, land acquisition purpose, not a construction bid opportunity.

👁Watchlist Triggers

WWTP Master Plan completion and RFP. The City is "working with a technical firm" on the Master Plan — watch for the Master Plan adoption at City Council, which will trigger a design RFQ for the actual WWTP upgrade (potential $300M+ construction program). This is the most consequential future procurement in the City.

Sports Complex bid advertisement. "Construction is to begin this year" (FY24-25) per budget narrative. A GC RFP or pre-qualification should have been issued or be imminent. Check the City's procurement portal and City Council agenda for contract awards.

Fire Training Facility design award (FY25-26). Watch City Council agendas for a Fire Training Facility design contract — this is the leading indicator before the construction bid.

Los Flores IWMF environmental permitting milestones. The landfill needs environmental review and permits before liner construction. Watch CalRecycle and RWQCB permit filings for the Los Flores site.

General Plan update adoption. The General Plan update is near completion. Adoption will trigger a wave of infrastructure master plan updates (Water, Wastewater, Streets, Parks) and associated consultant/engineer procurements.

Mid-Cycle Budget Review (June 2025). The City manager will bring amendments to the second-year budget. Watch for any new capital projects added or carryover adjustments — this may unlock previously unfunded projects.

2025-26 rate increases (Water/Wastewater). Rate increases approved Oct 2023 phase in Jan 2024 through 2026. Increased Enterprise Fund revenues will support expanded capital spending in FY25-26 — watch the Water Fund ($35.2M capital in FY25-26) carefully.
